How to Convert Word to PDF: Which Method Works Best? — Complete Guide

You've created the perfect document in Microsoft Word. Now it's time to share it. But sending a Word file means formatting can break, fonts can change, and anyone can edit your work.

Converting Word to PDF solves all these problems. But which method should you choose? This guide compares the most effective approaches, covering their strengths, weaknesses, and ideal use cases.

Why Convert Word to PDF?

Converting Word to PDF is one of the most common document tasks. Here's why people do it:

Scenario Why Convert to PDF
Preserve formatting Your layout stays exactly as designed
Prevent editing Others can't accidentally modify your content
Universal compatibility PDFs open on any device
Professional appearance PDFs look more polished
Smaller file size PDFs are often more compact
Print ready PDFs print exactly as shown

Method 2: Microsoft Word (Built-in)

Microsoft Word includes a built-in PDF export feature.

How it works:

  1. Open your Word document
  2. Click File > Save As or File > Export
  3. Choose PDF as the format
  4. Click Options to customize:
    • Page range
    • PDF quality (Standard or Minimum)
    • Include bookmarks
    • Embed fonts
  5. Click Save

Pros:

  • Free (with Office)
  • Works offline
  • Full control over settings
  • No extra tools needed

Cons:

  • Requires Microsoft Word
  • Limited to one file at a time

When to use: Simple documents, when you have Word installed.

Method 3: Google Docs (Free Cloud-Based)

Google Docs offers a free, browser-based way to convert Word documents to PDF.

How it works:

  1. Upload your Word document to Google Drive
  2. Open it with Google Docs
  3. Click File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf)

Pros:

  • Completely free
  • Works on any device with a browser
  • Cloud storage and sharing

Cons:

  • Formatting may change slightly
  • Privacy concerns (files uploaded to Google servers)
  • Complex layouts may not preserve perfectly

When to use: Quick conversions, non-sensitive documents.

Method 4: Adobe Acrobat Pro (Desktop)

Adobe Acrobat Pro offers the highest quality Word to PDF conversion with full control over settings.

How it works:

  1. Open Acrobat Pro
  2. Click Tools > Create PDF
  3. Select From File and choose your Word document
  4. Choose conversion settings:
    • Preserve formatting – Keep original layout
    • PDF/A compliance – For archiving
    • Compression – Reduce file size
  5. Click Create
  6. Save the PDF

Pros:

  • Highest quality output
  • Full control over settings
  • Works offline
  • Batch processing
  • PDF/A compliance for archiving

Cons:

  • Expensive (subscription required)
  • Installation required

When to use: Professional use, complex documents, sensitive files.

Method 5: WPS Office (Free Local Alternative)

WPS Office offers a built-in Word to PDF converter that processes files locally.

How it works:

  1. Open your Word document in WPS Writer
  2. Click File > Export to PDF
  3. Choose output settings
  4. Click Export

Pros:

  • Local processing — files stay on your device
  • Good formatting preservation
  • Free tier available
  • Works offline

Cons:

  • Requires installation
  • Advanced features may require subscription

When to use: Daily office work, sensitive documents.

Word vs PDF: Key Differences

Feature Word (.docx) PDF (.pdf)
Editable ✅ Yes ❌ No (without special tools)
Formatting consistency ⚠️ Can vary by device ✅ Always identical
File size ⚠️ Often larger ✅ Usually smaller
Universal viewing ⚠️ Requires Word or compatible app ✅ Opens in any browser
Printing ⚠️ May shift margins ✅ Prints exactly as shown
Security ⚠️ Easy to edit ✅ Can be password protected
