How to Convert PDF to Word: Which Method Works Best? — Complete Guide

You have a PDF that needs editing. But PDFs aren't designed for easy editing—Word documents are. Converting PDF to Word gives you the freedom to modify text, update tables, and reformat content effortlessly.

But which method should you choose? This guide compares the most effective PDF to Word conversion methods, covering their strengths, weaknesses, and ideal use cases.

Why Convert PDF to Word?

Converting PDF to Word is one of the most common document tasks. Here's why people do it:

Scenario Why Convert to Word
Edit text Fix typos and update information
Update content Change dates, prices, or names
Reuse content Copy and paste sections into new documents
Fix errors Correct mistakes in reports or contracts
Collaborate Use Word's track changes for team reviews
Save time No need to recreate documents from scratch

Understanding the Challenge

PDF to Word conversion isn't perfect. Here's why:

PDFs store content like a photograph. Every word, image, and shape has a fixed position on the page.

Word documents store content like flowing text. Words reflow when you edit, add, or delete content.

When a converter reads a PDF, it tries to guess where paragraphs start and end, which font styles map to Word styles, and how to rebuild tables. This guessing game is why conversions sometimes fail.

The result: Fonts change, tables break, paragraphs split into short lines, and images float away.

The good news: With the right tool and approach, you can achieve near-perfect conversions.

Method 2: Microsoft Word (Built-in)

If you have Word 2013 or later, you can open PDFs directly.

How it works:

  1. Open Word
  2. Go to File > Open
  3. Browse and select your PDF file
  4. Click OK when Word prompts you to convert
  5. Edit the document as needed
  6. Save as .docx

Pros:

  • Free (with Office)
  • Works offline
  • No extra tools needed

Cons:

  • Complex layouts may not convert cleanly
  • Scanned PDFs won't work (no OCR)
  • Formatting may shift
  • Limited to Windows (not available on Mac)

When to use: Simple text-based PDFs with basic layouts.

Method 3: Google Docs (Free Cloud-Based)

Google Docs offers a free, browser-based way to convert PDFs to Word.

How it works:

  1. Upload your PDF to Google Drive
  2. Right-click and select Open with > Google Docs
  3. Google Docs converts the PDF automatically
  4. Go to File > Download > Microsoft Word (.docx)

Pros:

  • Completely free
  • Works on any device with a browser
  • Built-in OCR for scanned PDFs
  • Cloud storage and sharing

Cons:

  • Formatting may not preserve perfectly
  • Privacy concerns (files uploaded to Google servers)
  • Manual cleanup often required

When to use: Quick conversions, non-sensitive documents.

Method 4: Adobe Acrobat Pro (Desktop)

Adobe Acrobat Pro offers the highest quality PDF to Word conversion with full control over settings.

How it works:

  1. Open the PDF in Acrobat Pro
  2. Click Export PDF in the right panel
  3. Select Microsoft Word as the export format
  4. Choose Word Document or Word 97-2003 Document
  5. Click Export and save the file

Pros:

  • Highest quality output
  • Full control over formatting
  • Built-in OCR for scanned documents
  • Works offline
  • Batch processing

Cons:

  • Expensive (subscription required)
  • Installation required

When to use: Professional use, complex layouts, sensitive documents.

Method 5: WPS Office (Free Local Alternative)

WPS Office offers a built-in PDF to Word converter that processes files locally.

How it works:

  1. Open the PDF in WPS Office
  2. Click Convert in the top toolbar
  3. Select PDF to Word
  4. Choose output settings
  5. Click Start Conversion

Pros:

  • Local processing — files stay on your device
  • Good formatting preservation
  • Built-in OCR for scanned PDFs
  • Free tier available

Cons:

  • Requires installation
  • Advanced features may require subscription

When to use: Daily office work, sensitive documents.

Common Conversion Problems and How to Fix Them

Problem 1: Formatting looks wrong

Solution: Try a different conversion mode or tool. "Preserve layout" mode usually gives the best results.

Problem 2: Paragraphs are broken

Why it happens: Each line break becomes a paragraph mark.

How to fix it:

  1. Press Ctrl + H to open Find & Replace
  2. Find: ^p, Replace with: space
  3. Click "Replace All"

Problem 3: Text is in text boxes

Why it happens: The converter preserved exact layout using text boxes.

How to fix it:

  1. Press Ctrl + A to select all
  2. Press Ctrl + X to cut
  3. Open a new blank Word document
  4. Right-click and choose "Keep Text Only"

Problem 4: Scanned PDF won't convert

Solution: Use OCR first. AIPDFLY's OCR Tool can extract text from scans.
