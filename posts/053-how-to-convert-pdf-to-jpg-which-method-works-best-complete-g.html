How to Convert PDF to JPG: Which Method Works Best? — Complete Guide

You need to share a PDF as images. Maybe you're posting on social media, embedding pages on a website, or just want to preview content without sending the full file. Converting PDF to JPG is the answer, but which method should you choose?

This guide compares the most effective PDF to JPG conversion methods, covering their strengths, weaknesses, and ideal use cases.

Why Convert PDF to JPG?

PDF to JPG conversion is one of the most common document tasks. Here's why people do it:

Scenario Why Convert to JPG
Social media Upload images directly to Instagram, LinkedIn, or Facebook
Website content Display PDF pages as images on blogs or landing pages
Email previews Send image previews instead of large PDF attachments
Presentations Extract slides as images for use in PowerPoint or Keynote
Archiving Preserve pages as images for long-term storage
Mobile sharing Images are easier to view on phones than PDFs

PDF to JPG vs Screenshots

Many people take screenshots of PDFs. This is the worst way to do it.

Feature PDF to JPG Tool Screenshot
Quality Original resolution Screen resolution (low)
Speed Seconds per document Minutes per page
All pages Yes, automatically No, one at a time
Accuracy 100% page capture May miss edges
File size Optimized Often too large

Winner: PDF to JPG wins on every metric.

JPG vs PNG: Which Format to Choose?

Many people ask about JPG vs PNG when converting PDFs to images .

Use Case Format
Social media, email, sharing JPG (smaller file size)
Websites, previews JPG (faster loading)
Printing, archiving PNG (lossless quality)
Text-heavy pages, diagrams PNG (sharper text)
Photos, images with gradients JPG (better compression)

Recommendation: Use JPG for most sharing needs. Use PNG when text clarity or transparency is critical .

Understanding Quality: The DPI Factor

The single most important factor in PDF to JPG quality is DPI (dots per inch) .

DPI Setting Best For File Size
72 DPI Web previews, thumbnails Small
150 DPI Email, screen viewing Medium
300 DPI Printing, professional use Large
600+ DPI Archiving, high-precision Very large

The tradeoff: Higher DPI means better quality but larger files. Most tools default to 72-96 DPI, which is fine for screens but bad for print.

Method 2: Adobe Acrobat Pro (Desktop)

Adobe Acrobat Pro offers the highest quality PDF to JPG conversion with full control over settings.

How it works:

  1. Open the PDF in Acrobat Pro
  2. Go to File > Export To > Image > JPEG
  3. Click the Settings button
  4. Set resolution to 300 DPI or higher
  5. Enable "Use supersampling to improve image quality" for best results
  6. Click Export and choose a folder

Pros:

  • Highest quality output
  • Full control over DPI (up to 300+)
  • Batch conversion
  • Works offline
  • Super-sampling for quality enhancement

Cons:

  • Expensive (subscription required)
  • Installation required

When to use: Professional use, complex layouts, sensitive documents.

Method 3: Preview (Mac) — Built-in Tool

Mac users can use Preview for quick PDF to JPG conversion.

How it works:

  1. Open your PDF in Preview
  2. Click File > Export
  3. Choose JPEG as the format
  4. Adjust quality slider (push to Best for high quality)
  5. Click Save

Pros:

  • Free with Mac
  • Simple and fast
  • Works offline

Cons:

  • One page at a time (unless you export all pages)
  • Limited DPI control
  • Quality depends on slider, not DPI

When to use: Mac users, quick single-page conversion.

Method 4: Windows (Print to Image)

Windows users can use the "Microsoft Print to PDF" or third-party tools.

Alternative: Use Microsoft's built-in "Print to Image" feature.

How it works:

  1. Open the PDF in any viewer
  2. Open Microsoft Paint
  3. In the PDF viewer, click Print
  4. Select Microsoft Print to PDF (adjust options)
  5. "Print" as an image, then paste into Paint

Pros:

  • Free with Windows
  • No additional software needed

Cons:

  • Manual process
  • Lower quality
  • One page at a time

When to use: Emergency situations, no other tools available.

Method 5: Google Slides (Free Cloud-Based)

Google Slides offers a free, browser-based way to convert PDFs to images.

How it works:

  1. Upload your PDF to Google Drive
  2. Right-click and select Open with > Google Slides
  3. The PDF opens as a presentation
  4. Download each slide as image (File > Download > JPEG image)

Pros:

  • Completely free
  • Works on any device with a browser
  • Built-in OCR for scanned PDFs

Cons:

  • Manual process (one image at a time)
  • Privacy concerns (files uploaded to Google servers)
  • Limited quality control

When to use: Quick conversions, non-sensitive presentations.

Pro Tips for Best Results

Tip Why
Use high DPI for printing 300 DPI gives professional quality
Use lower DPI for web 72-150 DPI loads faster
Use grayscale for text Smaller file size, looks crisp
Preview a page first Test settings on one page before converting all
Use ZIP download Easier to manage multiple images

Common Issues and Solutions

Issue Solution
Blurry images Increase DPI to 150 or 300; use High quality setting
Large file size Reduce DPI (72-150 for web); use grayscale color mode
Pixelated text Use higher DPI (300); ensure PDF text is vector-based
Wrong pages extracted Double-check page range selection
Format doesn't save Make sure you're saving as .jpg (not .jpeg)
